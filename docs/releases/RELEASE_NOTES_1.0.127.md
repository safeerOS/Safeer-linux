# Safeer Browser for Linux 1.0.127 · Safeer OS 0.4.85 (with Safeer Control 2.1.82)

**Safeer Browser on GTK 4.** The browser is rebuilt on GTK 4 and WebKitGTK 6.0. The Flatpak carries our own patched WebKitGTK 2.54.1 and libsoup 3.8.0, and Safeer DRM for protected video – it starts only after you click Play in Safeer DRM and never without the site's permission. Safeer DRM is now part of every package (.deb, Flatpak, AppImage) and is no longer downloaded in the background after start. Google's Widevine module is fetched automatically from Google's servers the first time you play protected video – you install nothing.

**Privacy like Firefox.** Tracking protection in three levels (Standard, Strict, Custom) and a shield to turn it off for one site; per-site permissions (camera, microphone, location, notifications, autoplay, protected content); choose your DNS-over-HTTPS provider; clear browsing data, also on exit. Unwanted pop-ups are blocked – sign-in and payment windows still open. A pop-up that opens empty and then jumps to another site is blocked too.

**A real browser.** Downloads to your Downloads folder with a question before running programs; find with a match counter; print; full screen; mute a tab; zoom; links to apps (e-mail, phone, magnet) open after you confirm; network and certificate error pages in six languages; offer to make Safeer your default browser.

**Media Center, redesigned.** A left menu with your real sources, a Home page with Continue watching, Recently added, Most played and Favourites, and a now-playing bar at the bottom. Automatic playlists from your own music (Most played, Not heard for a while, genres, artists, Discover) – made on your computer, no cloud. Hide what you don't use from the menu. Play on … sends music to a network speaker (DLNA) or to your devices in Safeer Link. Audio tracks, audio and subtitle delay, subtitles from a file or the web, equaliser. The film and series catalogue loads twice as fast; live TV shows every channel logo and RTV SLO plays again. Login tokens of your media servers (Jellyfin, Navidrome, Plex …) are never stored in playlists or sent to other devices; Jellyfin 12 is supported.

**One desktop, one process.** Safeer OS, Safeer Control, Safeer Link, the Media Center, the player and the web all run in one process; the desktop shell runs next to it in its own supervised process and comes back in under a second if it stops. At idle the whole desktop uses about 470 MiB on the test PC (each web chat you add in Messages, such as Gmail, adds its own web page: about 590 MiB measured for Gmail). The desktop speaks six languages, uses one colour theme, works with the keyboard and the TV remote, and now has everything the previous desktop had: file actions, Continue here, Show on …, voice and notes in search, largest files, dock editing. Notifications go one way, settings for Link, the web and the desktop are in Safeer OS Settings, and the desktop widget shows what is playing.

**Linux Mint.** The desktop no longer hides behind the Mint panel or the dock; a mouse applet in the panel replaces the misleading battery icon on desktop PCs.

**Our WebKit everywhere, and faster.** GTK 4 is the default everywhere, and the .deb packages now carry the same patched WebKitGTK 2.54.1 and libsoup 3.8.0 as the Flatpak. It is built with Clang 22, ThinLTO and profile-guided optimisation (PGO), and in the .deb also optimised with BOLT. On our test PC (Intel Core i5-4590), Speedometer 3.1 went from 8.2 with our earlier GCC build to 11.1–11.4 with PGO and 11.9 with PGO and BOLT; BOLT also adds about 2 % on JetStream and 3 % on MotionMark. Our WebKit fix stops pages with many `backdrop-filter` layers (for example www.24ur.com) from eating memory and a whole CPU core. WebKit has accepted it (324100@main) and it is in the WebKitGTK 2.54 branch, so it also reaches other WebKitGTK users with their next update.

**Tabs like a modern browser.** Tabs shrink instead of breaking the layout, show the site icon and the full title on hover, and sit in the window's title bar. Web pages follow the system's dark mode (you can change it).

**DNS without limits.** Safeer Shield is the default DNS (blocks ads and threats, never challenge pages such as Cloudflare or hCaptcha), with Quad9, Cloudflare, Google, AdGuard and OpenDNS over encrypted DNS, your own address, or off – and an automatic fallback if a provider fails.

**Your accounts in the Media Center.** Sign in to YouTube once (in Splet, several accounts) and your playlists are available on all your linked devices. Spotify playlists, liked songs and podcasts play in our player with our controls, and Safeer Shield mutes and skips audio ads; signing in with Google lets you pick the Google account. Twitch plays in our player without inserted ads. Sign out of any of them inside Safeer OS.

**A fuller Media Center.** Home now has discovery shelves (popular films and series, music, your YouTube and Spotify playlists, Twitch live, radio); YouTube plays inside the Media Center window; playlists are shown as track lists; search is much faster and shows one card per title with the best stream; cards show quality, language and source. Adult add-ons appear only in the Private category, only when you turn it on and only on that device. Play on … is also in the video window, and picture and sound stay in sync on network speakers (adjustable delay).

**Messages.** Archive, delete, mark read or unread, star, move or label right in the list, several at once, with Undo; reply from the list without opening the conversation. The messages panel in the top bar can mark a message read or unread, mark it important or delete it (with Undo).

**Desktop.** Safeer Browser instead of Firefox in the dock, one Show desktop button, distinct icons for Messages and Splet.

**Icons you can tell apart.** Safeer Shield now has its own icon in the top panel next to Messages: it shows whether the Shield is on, off or has a problem, with the number of blocked requests; click it for Shield settings, or turn it on and off from its menu. The Linux Mint update icon in the panel is now a circular arrow instead of a shield (with the Papirus and the Mint-Y icons), so it is no longer mistaken for Safeer Shield. Safeer Control shows a computer and a phone in the panel instead of a shield. Safeer Browser has its own icon (a globe with the Safeer S), so it is no longer the same as Safeer OS.


**Your own scripts in the Media Center.** In *Customize menu → My scripts* you can import JavaScript (.js) and shell (.sh) files yourself. JavaScript runs in the Spotify page when Spotify starts (off until you switch it on, no access to Safeer OS itself); a shell script runs only when you press Run, with your own permissions, for at most 60 seconds, and its output is shown. Safeer does not supply or review scripts; what you import is your responsibility, and you confirm a warning before every import.

**Spotify and ads.** Shield mutes an ad on a free account and the music carries on afterwards; Safeer no longer blocks or replaces Spotify's ad requests (that stalled the player after an ad). Spotify's terms forbid avoiding ads, and Safeer does not promise to hide them.

**Small fixes.** Settings search finds *Network connections* when you type »wifi« and hides the volume slider like every other setting that does not match. The update note on Home shows only when there is an update. Hidden Safeer OS windows no longer refresh the clock, the Shield counter, sound, network, devices and the message list in the background; they catch up as soon as you show them.

**Hide shelves.** *Automatic playlists* and *Recently added* on Home can be hidden for good (button *Hide*, bring them back under *Customize menu*); hidden shelves are not loaded.

**First start and the way back.** A new Safeer OS installation opens a short guide: desktop language (six languages, the system language by default), optional sign-in to YouTube, Spotify and Twitch on their own pages, Safeer Shield, and linking a device with Safeer Link – every step can be skipped, and the guide is in Settings to run again. Existing users do not get it on update. *Back to Cinnamon* in Settings (or `safeer-os --nazaj-na-cinnamon` in a terminal) stops Safeer OS from starting at sign-in and restores the Mint panel, dock and look you had before – no uninstalling, no password, your data stays; opening Safeer OS from the menu offers to bring it back.

**Stability.** Idle and unanswered network connections are closed after 10 seconds, so they no longer pile up and crash the desktop; Safeer Link keeps listening even after a temporary error. The desktop no longer stutters every 10 seconds when no Hub is connected, and starting a magnet stream, a handover or loading dock icons no longer freezes it. The side dock comes back by itself if it closes or crashes (at most three times in ten minutes). Network speakers keep playing when the TV turns on and grabs the speaker by itself (HDMI-CEC) – if you switch to the TV yourself, your choice wins. JetStream 3.0 runs to the end again: web pages now see your real language instead of »C«, and a page may use up to half of your RAM (was a quarter) – the memory guard now stops only a page whose memory keeps growing (a real leak), not a demanding page with a short peak.
